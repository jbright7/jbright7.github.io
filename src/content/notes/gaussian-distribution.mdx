<Infobox title="Normal distribution">

**Parameters:** mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$, standard deviation $\sigma > 0$

**Support:** $x \in \mathbb{R}$

**Mean, median, mode:** $\mu$

**Variance:** $\sigma^2$

</Infobox>

The **Gaussian**, or normal, distribution is the bell-shaped curve that appears
whenever many small, independent effects add up.[^clt]

## Definition

<Callout kind="definition">

A random variable $X$ is normally distributed, written $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$,
if its probability density is

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).
$$

</Callout>

## Why it shows up everywhere

The short answer is the [central limit theorem](/notes/central-limit-theorem/),
which says that sums of many independent random variables tend toward a Gaussian.

[^clt]: Under mild conditions on the individual effects; see the central limit theorem note.
